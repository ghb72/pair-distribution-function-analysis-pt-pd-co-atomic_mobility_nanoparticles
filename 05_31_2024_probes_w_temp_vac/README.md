# Temperature and Vacuum Probes (05_31_2024)

## Purpose

Investigation of temperature effects and vacuum conditions on nanoparticle structure. This study explores how thermal treatment and environmental conditions affect the final PDF signatures.

## Physical Context

### Temperature Effects
Temperature influences:
- Atomic mobility and diffusion
- Phase transitions
- Surface reconstruction
- Ordering/disordering

### Vacuum Conditions
Vacuum environment affects:
- Surface energy minimization
- Absence of oxidation
- Free surface relaxation
- Atomic rearrangement kinetics

## Parameters Explored

1. **Temperature range**: Likely from 0 K (energy minimization) to elevated temperatures
2. **Equilibration protocols**: Different cooling/annealing schedules
3. **Vacuum conditions**: Isolated nanoparticle (no periodic boundaries)

## Probe Organization

Similar to `05_17_2024_probes_w_last_model/`, probes represent:
- Different temperature conditions
- Various equilibration times
- Statistical replicates

## Expected Observations

- **Low T**: More ordered structures, sharper PDF peaks
- **High T**: Disorder, peak broadening, possible phase changes
- **Vacuum**: Surface reconstruction, different from bulk behavior

## Comparison to Previous Work

This extends `05_17_2024_probes_w_last_model/` by adding:
- Temperature as variable
- Vacuum boundary conditions
- Thermal history effects

## Typical Analysis

1. Generate structure at reference temperature
2. Heat/cool with controlled protocol
3. Allow vacuum relaxation
4. Calculate PDF
5. Compare temperature-dependent changes

## Key Results

Temperature-dependent PDF changes reveal:
- Thermal expansion (peak position shifts)
- Disorder (peak broadening)
- Structural transitions (peak appearance/disappearance)

## Usage

Results here inform optimal synthesis/processing conditions for experimental validation.
